## Purpose

Sustain pressure before open conflict. The scene should feel unstable because characters
notice risk, conceal intent, or wait for a boundary to be crossed.

## Scene Logic

- Ground tension in a concrete source already present: missing information, suspicious
  behavior, unequal power, a deadline, physical vulnerability, or an unresolved event.
- Show selective attention. Characters track exits, hands, phones, tone changes, delayed
  replies, objects out of place, or who can overhear.
- Let dialogue carry double meanings, omissions, tests, and controlled corrections.
- Preserve spatial geography and plausible reaction time.
- Escalate by one observable step at a time: a question becomes specific, distance
  closes, an exit is blocked, evidence appears, or restraint visibly costs more.

## Restraint

- Do not reveal hidden truth without evidence available in the scene.
- Do not jump straight to violence, confession, or villain monologue.
- Avoid generic atmosphere detached from character decisions.
- End at a sharpened uncertainty, exposed inconsistency, or immediate boundary choice.
